Tenura Privacy Policy

Version 1.6 — last updated 30 October 2026

Tenura is a tenancy-agreement analysis tool. You paste or upload a tenancy agreement and it returns flagged clauses, missing protections and questions to ask before you sign. You can use it as a guest, or create an account to keep the analyses you run. This policy explains what personal data each of those involves, why we use it, who else sees it, and what you can ask us to do about it.

We have tried to write this in plain language rather than legal padding. If anything is unclear, ask us — the contact details are at the end.

3. What we collect

3.1 The document you submit

This is the heart of it. When you paste text or upload a file, we take the text of the tenancy agreement and analyse it.

As a guest, nothing about your document is kept. The result is returned to your browser and then forgotten: no copy of the text and no copy of the analysis is stored on our side. (To let you keep a result if you sign up straight afterwards, your own browser may briefly hold the text you typed — see section 3.2.)

When you are signed in, we keep the analysis and the document text behind it, so you can reopen it from your history later. You can delete any saved analysis yourself from the history page, which removes the text for good.

  • We keep the extracted text of the document. If you upload a PDF, Word file or photo, we keep the text we pulled out of it.
  • We do not keep your uploaded file. Uploads are read into memory, converted to text, and never written to disk as files.
  • Your filename is not stored. It is used only at the moment of upload to work out which file format you sent.

A tenancy agreement is a document full of personal data, and almost none of it is ours to ask for. It typically contains names, postal addresses, phone numbers and email addresses, the landlord's or agent's details, rent and payment amounts, sometimes bank or account details, and signatures. All of that arrives simply because it is inside the document you sent us.

Please do not upload a document you are not entitled to share. If it contains information about someone else — a landlord, an agent, a co-tenant — you need a lawful reason to give it to us, and to let us process it as described here.

We also reject anything that is not a tenancy agreement. If you upload a receipt, a photo, a different kind of contract or an unreadable file, we tell you so and we do not store it at all. No record of a rejected upload is kept beyond the ordinary server logs described below.

3.2 Accounts, and what we still do not collect

An account needs an email address and a way to sign in — a password, or your Google account — and nothing else. We do not ask for your name, phone number, date of birth, or address.

  • Passwords are hashed with Argon2id before they are stored. We never store the password itself and we cannot recover it. A forgotten password is reset through a one-time code emailed to you, not by us reading anything out.
  • One-time codes are stored hashed as well, expire after 10 minutes, can be used once, and are replaced when you ask for a new one.
  • A session cookie, named tenura_session, holds your signed-in state. It is set httpOnly (JavaScript on the page cannot read it), Secure, and SameSite=Lax, and it contains only a signed user reference and an expiry — no password, no document data. Signing out clears it.
  • If you sign in with Google, we receive your email address and a Google account identifier, and nothing else — no password is ever created for you. The sign-in happens on Google's own page: our page does not load Google's script and Google sets no cookie on our site. For the few minutes the sign-in takes we set one further short-lived cookie, tenura_oauth, which carries the anti-forgery value Google must echo back; it holds no personal data, cannot be read by page scripts, and expires after ten minutes. Google can, of course, see that you signed in to Tenura, under its own privacy policy.
  • No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no third-party trackers. No external scripts load on the page at all.
  • No payment details. The Service is free and takes no payments.
  • No location data, contacts, or device identifiers beyond what any web server sees in a standard request.
  • If you analyse as a guest, the text you typed may be held briefly in your own browser's session storage, so that we can offer to save the result if you then create an account. It stays on your device, disappears when you close the tab, and is cleared the moment you save it or dismiss the offer. Uploaded files are never held this way.

3.3 Technical data in logs

Like any web service, our servers log the IP address a request came from, the time, the path requested and any error trace. We deliberately keep document text out of the logs. The analysis path no longer logs clause text or the model's raw answers, and a redaction step strips email addresses, one-time codes and long token-like strings from anything that is logged. Logs are used for debugging and security only.

One thing that is never logged: one-time codes, passwords, and the text of your agreement. None of them is written to a log, and codes and passwords are never stored in a form anyone can read.

4. Why we are allowed to use it

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 ("NDPA") sets out the lawful bases for processing. We rely on:

  • Performing the service you asked for — analysing the document you submit and returning your results, and, where you have an account, signing you in and keeping your history. This is the main basis for the document text, the analysis, and your account details.
  • Our legitimate interests — keeping the Service working and secure, diagnosing failures, and preventing abuse. We keep this as narrow as we can, and it never overrides your rights.
  • Consent — where you specifically give it, such as when you write to us asking us to keep information, or where the law requires consent.
  • Legal obligation — where we are required to keep or disclose something by law.

5. AI processing: what actually leaves our server

This is the part most privacy policies mumble through, so here is the detail.

To judge a clause, we send that one clause's text and the relevant Nigerian rental norm to an AI model operated by Rumpty Cloud ("Rumpty AI Inference") over an OpenAI-compatible API. We do not send your whole document for clause analysis, and we do not send one document as a single request.

There is one other case. Before analysing anything, we decide whether the input is really a tenancy agreement. Most files are decided by fixed rules with no AI involved. If, and only if, the answer is genuinely ambiguous, we send only the first 1,800 characters of the text to the model to have it decide. The same applies to working out which country's rules apply: most agreements are decided by fixed markers (the naira sign, Nigerian places and law names, or foreign equivalents), and only a genuinely ambiguous one sends the first 1,500 characters to the model, which returns NG, NON_NG or UNKNOWN and nothing else.

Much of the analysis involves no AI at all. Known high-confidence traps — such as a re-entry clause that operates "whether or not lawfully demanded", or a non-refundable caution fee — are caught by fixed rules in our own code, and when a rule fires we do not ask the model about that clause. Missing protections and the questions to ask are worked out entirely by fixed code.

Two things follow from this that you should know:

  • Clause text can contain personal data — names, addresses, rent figures — and text we send to the AI provider therefore may too. It is sent for the purpose of evaluating the clause, and nothing else.
  • AI output is a suggestion, not a finding. Every flag that comes from the model must quote exact words from the clause, and our code verifies that the quote really appears there before showing it. AI-derived flags are labelled "[AI suggestion — worth a manual check]" in your results.

8. How long we keep it

Guest analyses are not stored at all, so there is nothing to keep.

For an account:

  • Saved analyses and their agreement text are kept until you delete them. You can delete any of them yourself from your history, and deletion removes the agreement text permanently and immediately.
  • Your account — the email address, the password hash (if you set a password; an account created with Google has none) and the Google account identifier (if you signed in with Google) — is kept while the account exists. To close it, delete your saved analyses and email us to remove the account itself.
  • One-time codes expire after 10 minutes, and are marked spent as soon as they are used. They are never reused.
  • Server logs are kept only as long as they are needed for debugging and security.

There is no automatic expiry on saved analyses. We would rather say that plainly than claim a retention period we do not enforce — and it is the reason the delete button is in your hands rather than ours.

To close your account, or to have anything else deleted, email privacy@tenura.online from the address on the account.

9. How we protect it

  • In transit over HTTPS.
  • At rest in a managed PostgreSQL database operated by Neon.
  • Uploads are handled in memory and are not saved to disk as files.
  • Passwords are hashed with Argon2id and are never stored or logged in plain text. Signed-in sessions use a signed, expiring cookie that page scripts cannot read, and no sign-in token is kept in browser storage where a script could reach it.
  • One-time codes are hashed, single-use, short-lived and attempt-limited.
  • Every account can reach only its own records — checked on the server for every query, not merely hidden in the interface.
  • Sign-in, sign-up and code requests are rate-limited to slow down guessing and abuse.
  • Access is limited to the people who operate and maintain the Service.

No system is perfectly secure and we do not pretend otherwise. Tenura is an early-stage project, not a bank. If you are uncomfortable sending a particular document, consider redacting the parts that are not relevant to the clauses you want checked — the names, phone numbers and account details — before you submit it. The analysis works fine without them.

  • Erase your data — see section 8.
  • Restrict or object to certain processing.

  • Withdraw consent at any time, where consent is what we relied on.
  • Complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC).

To exercise any of these, email privacy@tenura.online. We aim to respond within 30 days and will not charge you for a reasonable request.

One honest note on access and erasure: if you have an account, the fastest route is to manage your own data — your history lists what we hold against you and lets you rename or delete any of it directly. If you used Tenura as a guest, nothing was stored, so there is nothing to retrieve or erase beyond ordinary server logs. For anything else, email us; if we need more from you to find a record, we will say so.

11. Automated decisions

Your results, including the risk band, are generated automatically. That band is a rough indicator built from how many clauses were flagged, how many expected protections were missing, and how much of the agreement we were actually able to check. When too little could be checked, we say so and label the result "Review needed" rather than guessing — we do not present a thin analysis as low risk. It is not a legal assessment of your agreement and it is not a decision *about you*.

The NDPA restricts decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects or similarly significantly affect you. Tenura does not make that kind of decision. It gives you information about a document, and the choice of what to do with it — sign, renegotiate, walk away, take it to a lawyer — remains yours. Please treat every flag as a prompt to look, not a verdict.
